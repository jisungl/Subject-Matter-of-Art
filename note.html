Methodological Note

Note on Data

Sources & Construction

The dataset was extracted from joining two sources, objects.csv and objects_terms.csv, from the National Gallery of Art’s open data. objects.csv contains one row per object (144,192 total) with standard metadata—title, classification, medium, creation date, and so on. objects_terms.csv contains curated tags assigned by NGA cataloguers (568,518 total, since each object can carry multiple tags) describing the theme, school, keywords, and other attributes.

The two were joined on object ID, keeping only works that had both a theme tag and a school tag. Minor themes that collectively accounted for less than 10% of tagged works were dropped to keep the analysis focused. The final dataset contains 58,234 rows representing 51,842 unique objects—some works carry multiple theme tags—spanning the 1400s to 2000s.

What Was Lost

The primary losses from the original data were prints—of which only 40% had theme coverage—and Index of American Design objects, which had just 9% theme coverage overall. Specifically, the final dataset focuses on paintings, drawings, sculptures, and photographs, where cataloguing was most complete.

Limitations

The dataset is still sound for analysis, because the objects that were lost are not random. Inherently, what was dropped were works the NGA’s own cataloguers had not fully classified by theme. This likely means they are works where the subject matter is ambiguous, not yet reviewed, or not applicable. The surviving objects are the ones the gallery itself considers well-described.

Given the datasets provided by the NGA, it is impossible to remove all limitations created by each object in the gallery not having a theme or school tag. Consequently, some skewing and small inaccuracies cannot be resolved given the research question and the nature of the NGA datasets.
